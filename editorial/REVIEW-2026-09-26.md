# Promise content review — 26–27 Sep 2026

## How the checking was done

Round 1 (26 Sep) checked each record against its own source. Round 2 (27 Sep)
rechecked the 116 records that were contradictory, reading several kinds of
source for each:
- **Primary:** the politician's own words (pmindia.gov.in transcripts, Budget
  speeches) and official data (PIB, PRS, ministry portals, Supreme Court
  judgments).
- **Mainstream reporting:** PTI via ThePrint and Deccan Herald, Business
  Standard, The Tribune, Scroll.
- **Critical and right-leaning outlets:** The Wire and People's Democracy on
  one side, OpIndia and Swarajya on the other.
- **Fact-checkers:** BOOM and Alt News.

Where outlets disagree about what something means, the page now quotes each
reading with its source and settles the facts from the primary record. The
Hindu, Indian Express, NDTV, the Times of India group, HT, Moneycontrol,
India Today and the Wayback archive were not reachable. So were web search and
narendramodi.in, which is blocked by robots.txt.

The fixes are in `editorial/2026-09-26.json` and `editorial/2026-09-27.json`.
Apply them in date order with `python3 scripts/apply_editorial.py <file>`.

## p003 (₹15 lakh)

The editor was right: Modi did say it at a rally. At Kanker, Chhattisgarh, on
7 Nov 2013 he said: "If even once, the money hoarded by these crooks in banks
abroad, even if we bring only that back, every poor Indian would get free of
cost Rs 15 to Rs 20 lakhs, just like that". BOOM and OpIndia print the same
English rendering.

The words do not mention bank accounts, deposits or a date. BOOM rated only the
"deposit ₹15 lakh in every account" version as false. OpIndia, from the right,
reads the line the same way BOOM does, so BOOM was not an outlier.

Congress calls it a promise. In 2015 Amit Shah called it a "jumla", saying
"15 lakhs will not be credited in anyone's account".

What changed on the record:
- The promise text now uses his words and figure, ₹15 to ₹20 lakh.
- `made_on` is now 7 Nov 2013 (it was a 2014 date).
- The role at the time is "BJP prime ministerial candidate".
- The verdict stays **broken**, because the stated result never happened.
- The page shows the quote, marked as an English translation, and both
  readings side by side.

## Time rules for promises with no deadline (27 Sep 2026)

Set by the editor and enforced in `promise_rules.py`, which the pipeline runs on every save:

- **3-year rule.** A promise with no deadline is judged 3 years after it was made. If it is still ongoing then, it becomes broken, and its history is marked "no-deadline rule". This moved **p009** (Uniform Civil Code, 2019) to broken.
- **5-year rule.** An open-ended promise can be marked kept only after 5 years of monitoring.
  - A delivered promise stays ongoing with a `monitoring` note until then.
  - The 3-year rule never breaks a promise that is being monitored.
  - It becomes kept automatically at 5 years.
  - This moved **p076** (Karnataka free bus passes, delivered June 2026) from kept to ongoing, monitored until June 2031.
- **Clock start.** The clock starts on `made_on` for records an editor has verified. For all other records it starts on the later of `made_on` and `reported_on`, because the pipeline's dates are often wrong and must not break a promise early.
